## What to do right after it happens

-   Rinse your mouth gently with warm water.
-   If there's bleeding, apply gentle pressure with clean gauze.
-   Use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek to reduce swelling.
-   Collect any broken piece of the tooth and bring it with you.
-   Avoid chewing on that side of your mouth.
-   Cover a sharp edge with dental wax or sugarless gum if it's cutting your tongue or cheek.

These steps manage the situation, they don't fix it. A same-day visit is still the right call even if the pain feels manageable.

## What kind of break are you dealing with?

The repair depends on where the break is and how deep it goes:

-   **Chipped or cracked enamel** — the outer layer is damaged but the pulp is untouched; usually treated with bonding or a crown.
-   **Fractured cusp** — a chewing point breaks off; often restored with a crown or onlay in one visit.
-   **Cracked tooth** — a crack running toward the root; may need a root canal and crown, or extraction if it reaches below the gumline.
-   **Split tooth** — the tooth has separated into two pieces; part may be savable, or extraction and an implant may be needed.

A clinical exam and digital X-ray are the only way to know which one you have. That's the first thing that happens when you come in.

## Can it be fixed the same day?

For most fracture types, yes. Simple chips and fractured cusps are commonly bonded or crowned the same visit. Even cases that need a root canal are frequently completed in a single appointment at Dr. Smile's Lomita office, using digital imaging to assess the damage as soon as you arrive.

Same-day treatment isn't just about convenience — it limits how far the fracture can spread and reduces the window for infection to take hold. Waiting even a day or two gives bacteria more time to get into the tooth.
