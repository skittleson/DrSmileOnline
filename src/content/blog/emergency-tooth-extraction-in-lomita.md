## Signs You May Need an Emergency Extraction Right Now

Some situations cannot wait for a scheduled appointment. Watch for these warning signs:

-   Throbbing pain that gets worse over hours, not better
-   Visible swelling in the jaw, cheek, or neck
-   A bad taste or smell coming from around a tooth — a sign of active infection
-   Fever alongside dental pain, which suggests the infection is spreading
-   A tooth that is visibly cracked down to the gumline or hanging loose after an injury

## The Tooth Extraction Process: Step by Step

Understanding what happens during a tooth extraction in Lomita takes a lot of the fear out of it.

-   Local anesthesia is applied: The area around the tooth is completely numbed before anything begins
-   The tooth is loosened: The dentist gently works the tooth free from the socket using specialized instruments
-   The tooth is removed: For simple extractions, this takes just a few minutes
-   Surgical extractions: Impacted or deeply broken teeth may require a small incision in the gum, done under local anesthesia
-   The site is cleaned: Any infection or debris is cleared from the socket
-   Gauze is placed: Light pressure helps a clot form, and bleeding stops
-   You receive aftercare instructions: What to eat, what to avoid, and when to follow up

Modern anesthesia makes tooth extraction in Lomita far more comfortable than most people expect. The anticipation is almost always worse than the procedure itself.

## What to Do Before You Arrive

If you are in pain and heading to Dr. Smile for an urgent extraction, these steps can help manage symptoms in the meantime:

-   For swelling: Apply a cold pack to the outside of your cheek in 10-minute intervals — do not apply heat
-   For pain: Take an over-the-counter pain reliever as directed on the label
-   For a suspected abscess: Do not try to drain it yourself — this risks spreading the infection
-   For a loose or broken tooth: Avoid chewing on that side entirely
-   For bleeding: Apply gentle pressure with clean gauze or a cloth

## What Happens After Extraction: The Recovery Timeline

Recovery from a tooth extraction in Lomita is straightforward when you follow the aftercare plan:

-   Day 1: Rest, keep gauze in place, avoid rinsing or spitting forcefully
-   Days 2–3: Swelling peaks, then begins to subside — continue cold packs as needed
-   Day 4 onward: Soft foods, gentle rinsing with warm salt water, avoiding straws
-   Week 1: Most patients feel significantly better; follow-up visit confirms proper healing

## Frequently Asked Questions :

### Q. What happens if you delay an emergency tooth extraction?

Delaying an emergency tooth extraction in Lomita almost always makes the situation worse. An untreated infection can spread to the jawbone, surrounding teeth, and in serious cases, into the neck or bloodstream — a condition called Ludwig’s angina, which requires hospitalization.

### Q. Is tooth extraction in Lomita painful?

No,  not during the procedure. Local anesthesia ensures the area is fully numb before anything begins. Most patients feel pressure but not pain. Post-procedure soreness is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief and the aftercare steps provided by Dr. Smile’s team.

### Q. How do I know if my tooth needs extraction or just a root canal?

This depends on how much of the tooth structure remains and whether the infection is controllable. An emergency dentist in Lomita at Dr. Smile will take digital X-rays and evaluate the damage before recommending either option. The goal is always to save the tooth if possible.

### Q. How soon can I eat after a tooth extraction in Lomita?

Stick to soft foods for the first 24 to 48 hours — think yogurt, mashed potatoes, and smoothies without a straw. Avoid anything hard, crunchy, or very hot. Most patients return to a normal diet within a week, depending on the complexity of the extraction.
