### 2\. Comprehensive Examination

Once the history is established, the dentist performs a thorough oral evaluation.

-   Visual inspection: Checking teeth, gums, tongue, and oral tissues for cavities, gum disease, or abnormalities.
-   Periodontal assessment: Measuring gum pockets to detect early signs of periodontal disease.
-   Occlusion check: Evaluating bite alignment and jaw function.
-   Diagnostic imaging: X-rays or digital scans may be taken to reveal hidden issues such as bone loss, impacted teeth, or decay beneath fillings.

### Q. What services does a family dentist provide?

Family dentists handle preventive care such as cleanings and fluoride treatments, restorative work like fillings and crowns, cosmetic procedures including whitening and veneers, and pediatric care for children. They also treat gum disease and refer patients to specialists when needed.

### Q. How often should I visit a family dentist?

Most patients should schedule visits every six months for check-ups and cleanings. Those with ongoing dental issues or chronic conditions may need more frequent appointments. Children should also follow the six-month schedule to monitor growth and prevent cavities.

### Q. What should I expect during my first appointment?

Your first visit usually includes a review of your medical and dental history, a full oral exam, and X-rays if necessary. The dentist will also perform a cleaning and discuss a personalized treatment plan based on your needs.

### Q. How does a family dentist handle dental anxiety?

Family dentists use gentle communication, explain procedures clearly, and may offer sedation for patients with severe anxiety. For children, they create a friendly environment with positive reinforcement. Relaxation techniques like deep breathing or listening to music are also encouraged.
