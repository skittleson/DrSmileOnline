## What is gum disease?

Before exploring how a dentist can help, it’s important to understand what gum disease is. Gum disease, also known as periodontal disease, is an infection of the tissues that hold your teeth in place. It usually starts with plaque buildup, a sticky film of bacteria that forms on teeth.

-   Gingivitis: The earliest stage. Gums may become red and swollen and bleed easily.
-   Periodontitis: A more advanced stage where gums pull away from teeth, bone loss occurs, and teeth may loosen.

Without treatment, gum disease in Lomita can lead to tooth loss and even affect overall health, as bacteria from the mouth can enter the bloodstream.

## How to take care of your teeth, post-operation: practical tips from a dentist in Lomita

In this section of the article, we shall explore ways you can protect your newly treated gums. Sit and take notes as this comes from an expert dentist in Lomita.

### 1\. Follow the dentist’s instructions carefully

-   Your dentist may provide specific aftercare guidelines depending on whether you had scaling, root planing, or surgery.
-   Always take prescribed medications (like antibiotics or pain relievers) exactly as directed.

### 2\. Maintain Gentle Oral Hygiene

-   Brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush.
-   Floss carefully to avoid irritating healing gums.
-   Use an antimicrobial mouthwash if recommended.

### 3\. Control Bleeding and Swelling

-   Minor bleeding or swelling is normal after treatment.
-   Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling.
-   Avoid vigorous rinsing for the first 24 hours.

### 4\. Eat a Soft, Balanced Diet

-   Stick to soft foods (soups, yogurt, eggs, and smoothies) immediately after treatment.
-   Avoid hard, crunchy, or spicy foods that may irritate gums.
-   Include vitamin-rich foods (like leafy greens and citrus) to support healing.

### 5\. Stay Hydrated and Avoid Irritants

-   Drink plenty of water to keep your mouth clean and moist.
-   Avoid smoking and alcohol, as they slow healing and increase gum disease risk.

### 6\. Schedule Regular Follow-Ups

-   Gum disease requires ongoing monitoring.
-   Dentists may recommend more frequent cleanings (every 3–4 months instead of 6).
-   Follow-up visits help track gum pocket depth and overall progress.

### 7\. Watch for Warning Signs

-   Persistent bleeding, swelling, or pain should be reported to your dentist.
-   Loose teeth or receding gums may indicate the disease is returning.

## Frequently Asked Questions:

### Q. What causes gum disease?

Gum disease usually starts when plaque builds up on teeth. If it isn’t cleaned away, it hardens into tartar and irritates the gums, leading to infection.

### Q. Is gum disease painful?

In the early stages, it may not hurt at all. You might just notice bleeding when brushing. As it gets worse, gums can feel sore, swollen, or tender.

### Q. Can gum disease be reversed?

Yes, if caught early. Gingivitis, the first stage, can be reversed with good cleaning and care. Advanced gum disease needs more treatment, but can be managed.

### Q. How often should I visit the dentist after treatment?

Most people need checkups every six months, but if you’ve had gum disease, your dentist may suggest visits every three to four months.

### Q. What can I do at home to keep my gums healthy?

Brush twice a day, floss daily, eat a balanced diet, and avoid smoking. These simple steps make a big difference in keeping gums strong.
