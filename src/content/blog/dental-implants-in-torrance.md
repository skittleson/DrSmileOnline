## What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone where the tooth root used to be. Once it bonds with the bone, a custom crown (or a full set of teeth) is attached on top. The result looks, feels, and functions like a natural tooth — and unlike a bridge, it doesn't rely on your neighboring teeth for support.

## Single-tooth vs. full-arch implants

### Single-tooth implant

-   Replaces one missing tooth.
-   Preserves the bone around the gap, which otherwise slowly shrinks.
-   Prevents the surrounding teeth from drifting into the space.
-   The most common implant case, and the one most people picture.

### Full-arch (All-on-4)

When several teeth or a whole arch are missing, individual implants can add up in cost and surgery. **All-on-4** uses a smaller number of strategically placed implants to support a full set of fixed teeth — a practical option for patients who've outgrown dentures. If you're weighing [**All-on-4 prosthetics**](/DrSmileOnline/all-on-four-prosthetics/), a consultation is the right place to find out if it fits your situation.

## Who is a good candidate?

Most healthy adults are candidates, but a few things are checked first:

-   **Bone volume** — enough jawbone to hold the implant, or a plan to build it up.
-   **Oral health** — active infection or gum disease is treated before implant work begins.
-   **Overall health** — certain conditions (uncontrolled diabetes, heavy smoking) affect healing and are discussed openly.

You don't need to "qualify" by having perfect teeth. You need an honest assessment of your bone, gums, and health — which is exactly what the consultation is for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q. How long does a dental implant take?

A single-tooth implant is staged: placement, a healing period while the implant bonds to the bone (often several months), then the crown. Full-arch cases vary. Your consultation will give you a realistic timeline for your specific case.

### Q. Are implants painful?

The placement is done under local anesthesia and most patients report it's comparable to, or less than, a tooth extraction. Discomfort after is typically manageable with standard pain relief.

### Q. How long do dental implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular checkups, implants can last decades — the titanium post is designed to be a long-term part of your jaw, and the crown can be replaced if it ever wears.

### Q. What if I don't have enough bone?

In many cases the bone can be rebuilt first with a graft. That's part of what the consultation imaging is for — to know whether you're ready now or need a preparatory step.
