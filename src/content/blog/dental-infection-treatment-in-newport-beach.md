## What Is a Dental Infection?

A dental infection happens when bacteria invade the soft tissue inside or around a tooth. This can stem from an untreated cavity, a cracked tooth, or advanced gum disease. Some infections develop slowly over months. Others seem to appear overnight. Either way, the underlying cause is always bacterial, and it always needs professional care.

Some very common signs include the following:

-   Persistent, throbbing pain that doesn’t go away
-   Swelling in the jaw, face, or neck
-   Fever or general fatigue
-   Sensitivity to hot and cold temperatures
-   A pimple-like bump on the gum (a dental abscess)
-   Difficulty swallowing or breathing — seek emergency care immediately if this occurs

Left untreated, an infection can spread to the jaw, neck, or even the bloodstream. That is why prompt dental infection treatment in Newport Beach is never optional.

## Frequently Asked Questions :

### Q. How do I know if I have a dental infection or just a regular toothache?

A regular toothache usually comes and goes. A dental infection tends to cause persistent, throbbing pain, swelling, and sometimes fever. If your pain is accompanied by any swelling or if you feel generally unwell, see a dentist right away.

### Q. Can a dental infection go away on its own?

No. Dental infections do not resolve without treatment. They may appear to improve briefly, but the bacteria remain. The infection will return and typically worsen over time.

### Q. Is root canal therapy the only option for a dental infection?

Not always. Mild infections may be treated with antibiotics and drainage. However, if the tooth pulp is involved, a root canal is usually necessary to fully eliminate the infection and save the tooth.

### Q. How does gum disease contribute to dental infections?

Infected gum pockets create pathways for bacteria to reach the tooth root. A deep cleaning dentist in Newport Beach can treat this underlying cause and reduce the risk of future infections.
