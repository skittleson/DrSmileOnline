## Signs You Need Emergency Dental Care

Dental problems can appear suddenly and cause serious pain. You can protect your smile by knowing the signs of a dental emergency. Urgent Dental Care in Newport Beach is available to handle dental problems quickly.

### Severe Tooth Pain

Severe tooth pain is one of the urgent signs that you need emergency dental care. If simple treatments don’t help the pain, it could mean an infection or damage. If you ignore it, it could get worse.

-   Pain that keeps you awake at night
-   Sharp pain when chewing or biting
-   Swelling around the tooth or gums

### Broken or Knocked-Out Tooth

Accidents can cause teeth to break or fall out. This is a true emergency because quick action can save the tooth. In many cases, a **[Best Dentist in Newport Beach](/DrSmileOnline/location/)** will be able to see you the same day.

-   A cracked or chipped tooth with sharp edges
-   A tooth that has been knocked out completely
-   Bleeding or swelling after injury

### Swelling and Infection

Swelling in the mouth, jaw, or face may indicate an infection. Dental infections spread quickly and may affect overall health. Urgent care prevents the infection from becoming dangerous.

-   Swollen gums or cheeks
-   Pus or bad taste in the mouth
-   Fever along with dental pain

### Lost Filling or Crown

If a filling or crown falls out, the tooth becomes weak and sensitive. Emergency dental care can replace it and stop further damage.

-   Sudden sensitivity to hot or cold
-   Visible hole or gap in the tooth
-   Pain when chewing

### Bleeding That Won’t Stop

Bleeding gums after brushing is common, but heavy bleeding that doesn’t stop is a warning sign. It may be linked to gum disease or injury.

-   Continuous bleeding after an accident
-   Blood mixed with swelling or pain
-   Difficulty eating due to bleeding

### Difficulty Opening the Mouth

If you suddenly find it hard to open your mouth or chew, it may signal a serious dental issue. This can be caused by infection, swelling, or injury to the jaw.

-   Trouble opening the mouth fully
-   Pain when chewing or speaking
-   Stiffness or swelling in the jaw
