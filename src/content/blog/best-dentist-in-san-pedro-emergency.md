## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1\. What should I do if I have a sudden toothache?

If you experience a sudden toothache, rinse your mouth with warm water and gently floss to remove any trapped food. Avoid placing aspirin directly on the tooth or gums. Contact your dentist immediately to identify the cause and get proper treatment.

### 2\. Can a dentist save a knocked‑out tooth?

Yes, if you act quickly. Place the tooth back in its socket if possible, or keep it moist in milk or saline solution. Visit a dentist right away—ideally within an hour—for the best chance of saving the tooth.

### 3\. How do dentists handle broken or chipped teeth?

Dentists can repair broken or chipped teeth using bonding, crowns, or veneers, depending on the severity. In emergencies, temporary solutions may be applied first, followed by permanent restoration.

### 4\. What qualifies as a dental emergency?

A dental emergency includes severe tooth pain, swelling or infection, broken or knocked‑out teeth, uncontrolled bleeding, or lost fillings and crowns that expose sensitive areas. These situations require immediate professional care.

### 5\. How can I prevent dental emergencies?

Regular dental checkups, good oral hygiene, and protective measures like wearing mouthguards during sports can reduce risks. Avoid chewing hard foods such as ice or popcorn kernels, and treat dental issues early before they become emergencies.

### 6\. How often should I visit the dentist for checkups?

Most people should schedule a dental checkup every six months. Regular visits allow dentists to catch problems early, provide professional cleanings, and offer preventive advice. In some cases—such as patients with gum disease, frequent cavities, or ongoing dental treatments—dentists may recommend more frequent visits to maintain oral health.
