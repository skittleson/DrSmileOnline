## Why Dental Emergencies Demand Immediate Treatment

A dental emergency is about protecting your teeth, gums, and overall health from a situation that can escalate quickly if left untreated. Most people wait too long. Here’s why this backfires:

-   A cracked tooth that is left untreated can split all the way to the root, making it impossible to save
-   An infected tooth does not heal on its own. The bacteria spread to the surrounding tissue, the jaw, and, in serious cases, deeper into the body
-   A knocked-out tooth has the best chance of being successfully reattached within 30 to 60 minutes of the injury
-   A lost filling or crown leaves the inner structure of the tooth exposed to bacteria and temperature, accelerating decay

Immediate dental treatment is not an overreaction. It is the responsible choice when your dental health is at risk. Reaching an emergency dentist in San Pedro as quickly as possible gives you the best possible outcome.

## What to Do Before You Reach the Emergency Dentist in San Pedro

The time between when a dental emergency occurs and when you reach the clinic matters. Here’s a clear breakdown of what to do for the most common situations:

For a knocked-out tooth

-   Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root
-   Do not scrub it or use soap. Rinse gently with clean water if it is dirty
-   Try to place it back in the socket if you can do so without forcing it
-   If that is not possible, store it in a small container of milk or clean water
-   Get to an emergency dentist in San Pedro within 30 to 60 minutes for the best chance of saving it

For a cracked or broken tooth

-   Rinse your mouth with warm water
-   Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek to reduce swelling
-   Avoid biting on that side entirely
-   Do not use any sharp object to remove broken pieces yourself

For a severe toothache

-   Rinse with warm salt water to reduce bacteria and soothe inflammation
-   Take ibuprofen to manage pain and reduce swelling
-   Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gum tissue, as it can cause a chemical burn
-   Call Dr. Smile as soon as possible for a same-day emergency appointment

For a lost crown or filling

-   Keep the area clean and avoid chewing on that side
-   Over-the-counter dental cement from a pharmacy can temporarily cover the exposed area until you are seen
-   Schedule your visit as soon as possible to avoid further damage to the tooth

For Signs of Infection or Abscess

-   Do not try to drain the abscess yourself
-   Rinse gently with warm salt water to ease discomfort
-   Take ibuprofen for pain management
-   Get emergency oral care immediately, as infections can spread rapidly and become medically serious
