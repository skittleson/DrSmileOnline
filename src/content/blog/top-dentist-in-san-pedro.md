## What is gum disease?

Gum disease, also known as periodontal disease, begins with gingivitis—mild inflammation of the gums caused by plaque buildup. If not treated, it progresses to periodontitis, where bacteria attack the tissues and bone supporting the teeth.

Common Symptoms

-   Red, swollen, or tender gums
-   Bleeding during brushing or flossing
-   Persistent bad breath
-   Receding gums
-   Loose teeth

A dentist in San Pedro can diagnose gum disease early and recommend scaling and root planing before the condition worsens.

### What is scaling and root planing?

-   An overview 

Scaling and root planing (SRP) is a specialized deep cleaning procedure that goes beyond the routine dental cleaning most patients are familiar with. 

-   How does it function?

While a standard cleaning focuses on removing plaque and tartar from the visible surfaces of the teeth, SRP targets the hidden areas beneath the gum line where harmful bacteria thrive. 

-   Implication 

This makes it one of the most effective treatments offered by a dentist in San Pedro for patients struggling with gum disease. 

### The step-by-step approach

Scaling: 

-   Removing Harmful Deposits
-   Scaling initiates the treatment by targeting buildup.

-   Dentists use specialized instruments to remove plaque and tartar (calculus) from tooth surfaces and below the gumline.
-   These rough, porous deposits harbor bacteria, causing gum irritation and inflammation; their removal promotes healing.

Root Planing: 

-   Smoothing the Foundation
-   Root planing is used to refine tooth roots.

-   Rough root surfaces allow bacteria to adhere, fostering reinfection; smoothing them prevents this.
-   Polished roots enable gums to reattach securely to teeth, forming a protective barrier against future bacteria.

## Recovery and Aftercare

Scaling and root planing are highly effective, but proper recovery and aftercare are essential to ensure long-term success. Patients who follow their dentist’s guidance can expect smoother healing and healthier gums.

### Immediate Aftercare

-   Mild Sensitivity: Teeth and gums may feel tender for a few days.
-   Pain Management: Over-the-counter pain relief may be recommended if discomfort occurs.
-   Diet Adjustments: Soft foods are best for the first 24–48 hours to avoid irritation.

### Oral Hygiene Practices

-   Gentle Brushing: Use a soft-bristled toothbrush to avoid damaging healing gums.
-   Flossing Carefully: Floss daily, but be gentle around sensitive areas.
-   Antibacterial Rinse: Your dentist may prescribe a rinse to reduce bacteria and promote healing.

### Lifestyle Considerations

-   Avoid Smoking: Tobacco slows healing and increases the risk of infection.
-   Limit Alcohol: Alcohol can irritate gums and interfere with recovery.
-   Stay Hydrated: Drinking water helps flush bacteria and supports gum health.

### Follow-Up Visits

-   Checkup Schedule: Dentists often recommend follow-up appointments to monitor healing.
-   Professional Cleanings: Regular cleanings help maintain results and prevent recurrence.
-   Progress Evaluation: Gum pocket depth may be measured to ensure improvement.
