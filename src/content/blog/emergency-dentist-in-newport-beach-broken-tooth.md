## What to Do Immediately After a Broken Tooth

Stay calm. Take a breath. Then follow these steps right away. A good emergency dentist in Newport Beach will work to see you the same day.

-   Rinse your mouth gently. Use lukewarm water — not hot, not cold. Extreme temperatures can worsen the pain if the nerve is exposed. This helps clean the area and remove any debris or blood.
-   Control the bleeding. Press a clean piece of gauze or a damp cloth against the area. Hold it for about ten minutes with light, steady pressure.
-   Don’t throw away the broken piece. If you can find the fragment, place it in a small container with milk or your own saliva. Keep it moist. Your **[dental clinic in Newport Beach](/DrSmileOnline/blog/best-dental-clinic-in-newport-beach/)** may be able to bond it back, depending on the break.
-   Manage the pain. An over-the-counter pain reliever like ibuprofen can help while you head to the clinic. You can also apply a small amount of dental cement from a pharmacy to cover any sharp or jagged edges temporarily.

## What to Do If a Tooth Gets Knocked Out

A completely knocked-out tooth is a true dental emergency. But don’t panic—knocked-out tooth treatment is possible, especially if you act within 30 to 60 minutes. Here’s what to do:

-   Pick up the tooth by the crown — that’s the white part you can see.
-   Do not touch the root.
-   Gently rinse it with water if it’s dirty, but don’t scrub it or use soap.
-   If possible, try to place the tooth back in its socket and hold it there gently.
-   If that’s not comfortable or possible, store it in milk or saliva, not water.
-   Get to an emergency dentist in Newport Beach as fast as you can.

## Broken Tooth Repair in Newport Beach — What Treatment Looks Like

Once you’re at the clinic, your dentist will assess the damage and recommend a treatment plan. Here’s what **[broken tooth repair in Newport Beach](/DrSmileOnline/blog/broken-tooth-repair-in-newport-beach/)** typically involves, depending on the severity.

-   Minor chips and cracks: These are the most common. If the nerve isn’t involved, dental bonding is usually the quickest fix. A tooth-colored composite resin is applied, shaped, and hardened — often in a single visit. It’s affordable, fast, and looks completely natural.
-   Moderate breaks: If a larger portion of the tooth has broken off, a dental crown is usually recommended. The crown covers the entire visible tooth, restoring its shape, function, and appearance.
-   Severe fractures:  When the break reaches the root or nerve, dental trauma treatment may involve a root canal before placing a crown. Modern techniques make this far more comfortable than most people expect. You’ll be numb, and it’s much less intimidating than it sounds.
-   Teeth that cannot be saved: In rare cases where the damage is too extensive, extraction may be necessary. From there, a dental implant or bridge can restore your smile fully.

## Tips to Prevent Broken and Knocked-Out Teeth

Once your tooth is repaired, protecting it is the next priority.

-   Wear a mouthguard during contact sports or any physical activity.
-   Avoid chewing on ice, hard candy, or pen caps.
-   Never use your teeth as tools to open bottles or packaging.
-   Visit your dentist regularly — small cracks caught early are far easier (and cheaper) to fix.
-   Ask your dentist about a nightguard if you grind your teeth in your sleep.
